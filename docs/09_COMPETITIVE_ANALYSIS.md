# Lumina — Competitive Analysis

**Last updated:** August 17, 2026

**Stage:** Private Beta / Production Testing

**Research type:** Competitor-owned websites and help centers

## Executive Summary

Lumina is entering a mature and competitive beauty-technology market. Booksy, Fresha, StyleSeat, Vagaro, and Square Go already combine professional discovery, profiles, services, prices, booking, and customer reviews. Several explicitly connect reviews to real or completed appointments.

This means **verified reviews alone are not a unique competitive advantage**.

Lumina's strongest current positioning hypothesis is the combination of:

- Trust-first beauty discovery
- Clear comparison before a client commits
- Portfolio, pricing, location, availability, and reputation in one profile
- A request-and-proposal flow for services that need conversation or custom details
- Completion-linked verified reviews
- The ability for professionals to keep using their existing booking tools
- A focused, premium experience for independent beauty professionals

Lumina should not compete head-on as a full salon operating system during private beta. The immediate opportunity is to determine whether clients and independent professionals value a clearer, more trustworthy decision layer before booking.

## Market Categories

### Direct marketplace competitors

These platforms help clients discover and book beauty or wellness professionals:

- Booksy
- Fresha
- StyleSeat
- Vagaro
- Square Go / Square Appointments

### Professional software competitors

These products primarily help professionals operate, market, schedule, and manage their businesses:

- GlossGenius
- Square Appointments
- Vagaro
- Fresha
- Booksy

### Existing customer substitutes

These are not identical products, but they already solve part of the problem for clients:

- Instagram and TikTok for visual discovery
- Google and Yelp for local search and reviews
- Facebook groups and local community pages
- Personal referrals and word of mouth
- Individual professional websites and booking links

The most important competitive question is not only, "Why Lumina instead of Booksy?" It is also, "Why would someone use Lumina instead of Instagram, Google, a referral, and an existing booking link?"

### Pricing snapshot

Pricing changes frequently and must be rechecked before using it in an investor, partner, or public presentation.

- **Booksy:** $29.99 per month for one user, plus $20 per additional user. Optional Boost currently charges 30% of a new Boost client's first visit.
- **Fresha:** $19.95 per month for the Independent plan or $14.95 per bookable team member for the Team plan. A marketplace-sourced new client currently carries a one-time 20% fee, with a stated minimum.
- **StyleSeat:** New Client Connection currently charges the professional 30% of the first appointment it supplies. Its client help center also lists a $2.35 booking fee.
- **Vagaro:** Its U.S. materials currently display a limited-time base price of $23.99 per month for one bookable calendar, with additional calendars priced separately.
- **Square:** Square Go is described as free for clients and businesses; paid Square tools and payment processing create the broader revenue model.
- **GlossGenius:** Current plans begin at $28 per month, with higher Gold, Platinum, and practice tiers.

## What the Research Changes for Lumina

### 1. Verified reviews are necessary, but not sufficient

Lumina's completion-linked review protection is an important trust requirement. However, Booksy, Fresha, StyleSeat, Vagaro, and Square Go all promote verified or post-appointment reviews.

Lumina should therefore avoid claims such as:

- "The only platform with verified beauty reviews"
- "Other beauty marketplaces do not verify reviews"

The stronger claim is that Lumina is designing the **entire decision experience** around clarity and trust, from discovery through completed-service reputation.

### 2. Request-first service matching may be a useful wedge

Most major competitors emphasize direct calendar booking. Lumina currently supports a structured request, professional proposal, client confirmation, conversation, external booking continuation, completion, and review flow.

This may be valuable for:

- Custom services that require consultation
- Flexible dates or times
- Mobile and travel-based professionals
- Services whose final price depends on the client's needs
- Professionals who want to accept or shape an appointment before it reaches their calendar

This is a hypothesis to test, not yet a proven advantage.

### 3. Working with existing tools reduces switching friction

Many professionals already use Square, GlossGenius, Booksy, Fresha, or another booking system. Lumina currently allows a professional to continue the client into an existing booking link instead of requiring Lumina to replace the full business system.

That creates a possible early message:

> Build trust and get discovered on Lumina while keeping the booking tools you already use.

The risk is that maintaining another profile may feel like extra work. Private-beta interviews must determine whether the added credibility and discovery are valuable enough to justify that effort.

### 4. Local marketplace density matters more than feature count

A marketplace is useful only when clients can find enough relevant, active professionals and professionals receive meaningful demand.

Lumina should prioritize:

- A focused launch area
- A small number of strong service categories
- Complete, credible professional profiles
- Responsive professionals
- Real completed-request and review history

Adding more features will not compensate for weak local supply or low client demand.

## Lumina's Current Strengths

- Clear trust-oriented product philosophy
- Database-protected request completion and review flow
- Structured professional profiles with portfolio, services, pricing, location, and availability
- Client comparison and saved-professional tools
- Request-linked messaging and notifications
- Flexible proposal flow before booking
- Support for salon, home-studio, mobile-salon, and travel-based professionals
- Ability to coexist with existing professional booking links
- Calm, premium visual direction
- Founder access to early users for close observation

These are product strengths. They are not yet proof of market advantage.

---

## Lumina's Current Weaknesses and Risks

- Very small marketplace supply compared with established competitors
- Limited client awareness and no proven acquisition channel yet
- No integrated calendar, payments, deposits, or no-show protection
- No established history of reviews or repeat transactions
- Professionals may not want to maintain another profile
- Clients may continue directly to social media or an outside booking site
- A request-first flow may feel slower than instant booking for simple services
- Trust positioning may be difficult to communicate without enough completed-service evidence
- Competitors already have significant marketplace, payment, and business-software advantages
- Lumina does not yet have a demonstrated moat

## Positioning Recommendation

### Recommended working position

> Lumina is a beauty discovery and trust platform that helps clients compare independent professionals with clarity before they commit—and helps professionals build credible discovery without replacing the tools they already use.

### What Lumina should emphasize

- Clarity before commitment
- Confidence in choosing the right professional
- Real work, transparent information, and verified service history
- Professional quality rather than follower count alone
- Flexible requests for services that require conversation
- Credibility for talented independent and newer professionals

### What Lumina should not claim yet

- That it is the largest or most trusted marketplace
- That its reviews are uniquely verified across the industry
- That it will automatically produce new clients
- That it replaces complete salon-management software
- That a final monetization model has been proven

## Potential Defensibility

Lumina does not currently have a strong defensible moat. Possible future defensibility could come from:

- Dense local supply and demand in selected markets
- A growing history of completed-service relationships and trusted reputation
- High-quality structured portfolio and service data
- Repeat client-professional relationships
- Strong local brand and community partnerships
- A request and consultation workflow that works especially well for independent beauty services
- Better matching or decision support based on real behavior and service outcomes

These advantages must be earned through usage; they cannot be created by product language alone.

## Private-Beta Research Priorities

### Professional interviews

Ask at least five professionals:

1. How do new clients currently find you?
2. Which booking and business tools do you already use?
3. What do those tools do well or poorly?
4. Would you maintain a Lumina profile without replacing your booking system?
5. What proof would make a Lumina lead feel qualified?
6. How do you feel about subscription, lead, and transaction fees?
7. Which part of onboarding feels like unnecessary work?

### Client interviews

Ask at least five clients:

1. Tell me about the last time you searched for a new beauty professional.
2. Where did you search first?
3. What made you trust or reject someone?
4. Which information was missing or difficult to compare?
5. Did you contact more than one professional?
6. Would you rather request details or instantly book?
7. What would make a "verified" review feel credible?

Avoid asking only whether people "like" Lumina. Ask about recent behavior, actual decisions, frustration, and money already spent.

### Competitor walkthroughs

Test at least three direct competitors as both a client and, where practical, a professional. Record:

- Signup time
- Profile setup effort
- Search and filtering quality
- Portfolio and pricing clarity
- Review labels and explanation
- Booking or request flow
- Fees shown during the experience
- Whether a professional can keep an outside booking system
- Confusing or surprisingly good moments

## Strategic Conclusion

Lumina should not try to win by having more operating features than Booksy, Fresha, Vagaro, Square, or GlossGenius.

The private-beta goal is to determine whether Lumina can become the clearest and most trusted **pre-booking decision experience** for a focused group of clients and independent beauty professionals.

The next competitive advantage should come from evidence:

1. Recruit credible professionals in one focused market.
2. Observe whether they complete and maintain profiles.
3. Watch real clients compare and send requests.
4. Measure confirmation, completion, review, and return behavior.
5. Learn which part of the trust lifecycle users value enough to return for.
