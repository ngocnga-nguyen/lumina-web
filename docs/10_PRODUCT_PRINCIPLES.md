# Lumina Product Principles

**Last Updated:** August 12, 2026

These principles guide product decisions. They are not a list of features.

## 1. Strengthen Trust

Lumina is a trust platform for beauty.

Every meaningful feature should make it easier for clients and professionals to trust one another. Trust should come from clear information, authenticated identity, real service history, transparent actions, and consistent product behavior.

Ask:

- Does this feature strengthen trust?
- Does it make an important claim more credible?
- Could it accidentally enable manipulation or misrepresentation?

## 2. Remove Decision Friction

The central client problem is not simply finding a professional. It is the fear of choosing the wrong one.

Lumina should reduce:

- Confusion
- Uncertainty
- Unnecessary effort
- Waiting
- Stress
- Disorganized communication

## 3. Make the Next Step Obvious

Users should know what to do without needing instructions from the founder.

Prefer clear status language, visible actions, helpful empty states, and predictable navigation. Avoid presenting several competing primary actions at once.

## 4. Build a Premium Experience, Not a Luxury Price Signal

Premium is a feeling created by:

- Thoughtful design
- Consistency
- Simplicity
- Attention to detail
- Customer care
- Trustworthy behavior

The interface should feel calm, clear, human, and considered—not expensive for the sake of appearing exclusive.

## 5. Support Better Versions of the User

Clients are not only requesting beauty services. They are seeking confidence, self-expression, care, and relationships with professionals they can trust.

Professionals are not only creating listings. They are building credibility, independence, a client base, and pride in their work.

Product decisions should respect both aspirations.

## 6. Work With Existing Professional Tools

Lumina should not prematurely replace full salon-management or booking systems.

Focus on discovery, trust, comparison, requests, communication, and reputation. Integrate with or link to existing booking tools when that removes friction.

## 7. Record Feedback Before Reacting

One person’s preference is an observation, not automatically a product requirement.

Log feedback first. Prioritize patterns that appear independently across multiple users, especially when they prevent users from completing a core task.

## 8. Protect the Core Lifecycle

The core product lifecycle is:

**Discovery → Request → Confirmation → Communication → Completion → Verified Review**

Changes should preserve the integrity, clarity, and security of this lifecycle.

## 9. Earn Complexity

Do not add major features, infrastructure, or operational burden before demand demonstrates a need.

Prefer the smallest reliable solution that validates the next important assumption.

## 10. Measure Value, Not Activity Alone

Views, clicks, and accounts are useful only when they lead to meaningful outcomes.

Prioritize evidence such as:

- Professionals completing credible profiles
- Professionals returning and responding
- Clients sending qualified requests
- Confirmed appointments
- Completed services
- Verified reviews
- Repeat client-professional relationships

## Product Decision Questions

Before building or significantly changing something, ask:

1. What problem are we really solving?
2. What hassle are we removing?
3. Does this strengthen trust?
4. Is this supported by repeated evidence or one opinion?
5. Does it improve the core lifecycle?
6. Can we test the assumption with a smaller change?
7. What outcome will tell us it worked?
